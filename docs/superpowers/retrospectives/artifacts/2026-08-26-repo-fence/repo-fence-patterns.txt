repo-fence.sh — the two classifier regexes, recovered 2026-09-09
================================================================

The hook itself (117 lines) and its 25-test harness were deleted on 2026-08-26
and the scratchpad copies did not survive. These two POSIX ERE patterns are all
that could be recovered, and they carry different confidence levels. Read the
confidence marker before using either.

The hook's decision was:

    BLOCK  iff  (VERB matches OR REDIR matches) AND the command text contains
                the fenced repo's path

That conjunction is the whole classifier. `analyze.py` reimplements it at its
`verdict = "BLOCK" if ((verb or redir) and names) else "ALLOW"` line.


REDIR — COMPLETE, verbatim
--------------------------
(^|[^0-9<>&])>>?[[:space:]]*[^|&[:space:]]

Matches `>` or `>>` not preceded by a digit or another redirection character,
followed by optional whitespace and a non-pipe target. Intent: catch shell
output redirection. Actual effect: also fires on `->` inside any quoted string
or comment, which is false-positive rows 10 and 11 of corpus.tsv.


VERB — PARTIAL, DO NOT TREAT AS THE SHIPPED PATTERN
---------------------------------------------------
(^|[;&|(]|[[:space:]])(git[[:space:]]+(commit|push|merge|...)|gh[[:space:]]+(pr[[:space:]]+(create|merge|...))|sed[[:space:]]+-i|tee|mv|cp|rm|mkdir|...)([[:space:]]|$)

The three `...` above are ELISIONS in the surviving transcription, not literal
regex content. The full alternation lists for the `git` subcommands, the `gh`
subcommands, and the trailing bare-command group are lost.

Consequence: re-running `analyze.py` against a hook rebuilt from this pattern
reproduces the FALSE POSITIVES faithfully (those come from REDIR and from the
`cp`/`rm` alternatives, all of which survive) but UNDERSTATES the false
negatives, because a shorter alternation list matches fewer commands. The
published result — 11 of 17 wrong, 7 false negative, 4 false positive — comes
from the run against the real hook on 2026-08-26 and cannot be regenerated.
